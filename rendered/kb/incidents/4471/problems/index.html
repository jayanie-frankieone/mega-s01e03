The parser fix will not stop the other two. Reports from booking-v3 forms and one-day moves will keep coming after it ships. Each problem now has its own ticket, linked in the table.

The three problems

parser: the shared parser reads dates month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first, so any date with a day of 12 or less comes out with day and month swapped. This is the bug #4471 describes, and it holds most of the reports.

hint: the booking-v3 form contradicts itself. Its date field prints MM/DD/YYYY, but the form reads what people type the tenant's way, day first. People follow the hint, and their date gets swapped. It only happens on the booking form of tenants that run booking-v3. Their app and email reports are parser.

one-day: the date moves by one day. These bodies do not swap day and month. The booking lands a day early, and where you are decides which day you see: one meeting showed as Tuesday in Lisbon and Monday in Toronto. That points at a time-zone or daylight-saving conversion, not at the parser. The desk already noted these reports (oneDayMove), but the #4471 page counts them under parser.

How this was counted

  • The export has duplicate rows. One id is one report.
  • A report's problem comes from its tenant and the way it came in (causes.ts), not from the intake tag. Intake guesses from the reporter's browser, and it got many of these wrong.
  • The one-day moves are the ids in desk-notes.json. Every other report that names two dates names an exact day/month swap.
  • Counts are the desk's own: the desk files the one-day moves under their tenant's cause, so the parser count includes them, as marked in the table. They are tracked in their own ticket too.
  • The table is computed from the data each time the page renders. If the desk merges more reports into #4471, the counts follow.

Why they were merged

Decision 0001 merges reports whose subject lines match, without reading the body. All three problems arrive with the same subjects ("Wrong day on the invite", "Date is wrong"), so they all landed in one ticket. Subject lines say how a problem looks, not what causes it.
